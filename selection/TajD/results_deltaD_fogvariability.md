# Results

## Population-level fog variability and inbreeding

The five populations with Tajima's D tracks spanned a roughly two-fold range in long-term fog variability. Detrended interannual SD of fog-season fog days/month (`fog_detrend`) ranged from 1.41 (Gaviota) to 1.79 (Pt. Arguello), with Tranquillon Mtn intermediate at 1.53; the coefficient of variation (`fog_CV`) ranged from 8.0% (Pt. Arguello) to 20.6% (Tranquillon Mtn) — the two metrics did not rank populations identically, reflecting that `fog_CV` is normalized by each site's mean fog level while `fog_detrend` is not. Mean inbreeding coefficient (ngsF *F*) ranged over an order of magnitude, from 0.023 (Tranquillon Mtn) to 0.193 (Gaviota), with the remaining three populations clustered between 0.10–0.11 (Pt. Conception, Pt. Arguello, Lion's Head).

## Delta Tajima's D across populations

For each population, Δ*D* (outlier-window Tajima's D minus that population's genome-wide background median) was positive for the general "Outliers" category in all five populations (range: +0.14 to +0.56), indicating that GEA-outlier windows generally sit above genome background in Tajima's D regardless of population (Fig. `TajD_delta_vs_ngsF.jpg`, `TajD_delta_vs_fogDetrend.jpg`). The "Putative inversion outliers" subset was more variable, ranging from −0.36 (Gaviota) to +0.80 (Pt. Arguello), and was the only category/population combination with a negative Δ*D* — i.e., Gaviota was the sole population where inversion-linked outlier windows fell *below* genome background.

Plotted against the inbreeding coefficient, Δ*D* showed no consistent monotonic pattern for either category: Gaviota (highest inbreeding, *F* = 0.19) had a positive Δ*D* for "Outliers" but the most negative Δ*D* for "Putative inversion outliers," while Tranquillon Mtn (lowest inbreeding, *F* = 0.023) had one of the largest positive inversion Δ*D* values (Fig. `TajD_delta_vs_ngsF.jpg`).

Plotted against detrended fog SD, both categories trended upward with increasing fog variability, and the "Putative inversion outliers" category showed a visibly steeper apparent slope and wider range than "Outliers" across the same five populations (Fig. `TajD_delta_vs_fogDetrend.jpg`): the lowest-fog-variability population (Gaviota) had the only negative inversion Δ*D*, and the highest-fog-variability population (Pt. Arguello) had the largest positive Δ*D* in both categories.

## Mixed-effects test

To test these apparent relationships while properly accounting for non-independence among the thousands of outlier windows within each population, a linear mixed-effects model (window-level Δ*D* ~ environmental covariate × category, with population-level random intercept and category slope) was fit separately for the inbreeding coefficient and detrended fog SD. This model was restricted to outlier windows from the "fog" GEA scan only (n = 12,699 windows across 5 populations), not pooled across all environmental variables as the population-median plots above are — the windows entering a test of the fog-variability relationship need to already be fog-associated outliers.

The inbreeding-coefficient model returned a singular fit (population-level random-effect variance could not be reliably partitioned) and its estimates were not interpreted further; its interaction term (estimate = −3.20, 95% CI −7.12 to 0.71, *p* = 0.080) is reported only for completeness.

The detrended-fog-SD model converged without singularity. The estimated slope of Δ*D* on fog variability was positive in both categories but did not reach conventional significance: +0.80 (95% CI −0.84 to 2.44, *p* = 0.22) for "Outliers" and +1.78 (95% CI −1.51 to 5.06, *p* = 0.18) for "Putative inversion outliers." The interaction term — testing whether the inversion category's slope differs from the general outlier category's slope — was positive but with a confidence interval comfortably including zero: difference = +0.98 (95% CI −1.37 to 3.32, *p* = 0.28) (Fig. `TajD_mixedmodel_vs_fogDetrend.jpg`).

Bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals on the individual population medians (2,000 resamples per population × category, computed from the same fog-restricted window set used in the mixed model) showed that Gaviota's negative "Putative inversion outliers" Δ*D* and Pt. Arguello's strongly positive values in both categories were the best-supported points in the dataset (narrowest CIs), while the three intermediate populations (Pt. Conception, Tranquillon Mtn, Lion's Head) had inversion-category CIs that included or nearly included zero (Fig. `TajD_mixedmodel_vs_fogDetrend.jpg`).

## Summary

Across all analyses, detrended fog variability — but not inbreeding coefficient — showed a directionally positive relationship with Δ*D*, with the putative inversion region trending toward a steeper apparent relationship than the genome-wide outlier set. This pattern did not reach statistical significance in the mixed-effects model (interaction *p* = 0.28), a limitation attributable to the small number of independent populations (n = 5) rather than to the amount of underlying genomic data.

Two corrections were made over the course of this analysis, each of which weakened the apparent effect relative to earlier, incorrect versions: (1) Tranquillon Mtn's covariates were initially computed from an incorrect 16-individual sample subset rather than the full 277-individual bamlist actually used to build its Tajima's D track (interaction *p* moved from 0.097 to 0.26); and (2) the mixed model initially pooled outlier windows across all environmental variables rather than restricting to fog-associated outliers specifically (interaction *p* moved from 0.26 to 0.28, and n from 17,522 to 12,699 windows). The qualitative pattern (positive slopes, steeper for the inversion category) survived both corrections, but the statistical evidence for it did not strengthen and should be considered suggestive rather than confirmatory given n = 5 populations.

## LD-expanded outlier windows

The GEA outlier SNP sets were subsequently regenerated, and outlier-window matching was extended beyond windows overlapping an outlier SNP directly to also include windows nearest a position in tight LD (r² ≥ 0.4, ngsLD, within 300kb, matching the r² threshold already used elsewhere in this project for SNP–gene linkage) with an outlier SNP, kept separate per environmental variable throughout. This increased matched windows roughly 10–20-fold (general "Outliers": ~390 to ~8,300–8,700 per population; "Putative inversion outliers": 21 to 211–215 per population) and let the fog-variability mixed model converge without the singular fit seen with the smaller, LD-unexpanded outlier sets.

The ridgeline distributions of Tajima's D across variables and populations (`TajD_extreme_all_ridge_inversion.jpg`) show a consistent qualitative pattern in all five populations: genome-background Tajima's D (grey) is unimodal and shifted left of zero, while every outlier track — across all three environmental variables (BIO18, BIO15, fog) and both the general and inversion-restricted subsets — is visibly shifted right relative to its own population's background and is often distinctly bimodal, with a secondary mode at or above zero that the background distribution lacks. This rightward shift is most pronounced in Pt. Arguello and Gaviota, the populations at either extreme of the fog-variability gradient, and comparatively muted in Tranquillon Mtn, where both background and outlier distributions are narrower and remain concentrated well below zero, consistent with its low nucleotide diversity.

The mixed-effects model on these LD-expanded windows (`TajD_mixedmodel_vs_fogDetrend.jpg`) quantifies this: Δ*D* increases with detrended fog-day-count variability in both categories, more steeply for "Putative inversion outliers" (slope = 1.33, 95% CI −0.25 to 2.91, *p* = 0.075) than for the general "Outliers" set (slope = 0.56, 95% CI −0.59 to 1.71, *p* = 0.22); the difference between the two slopes remains positive but short of conventional significance (0.78, 95% CI −0.51 to 2.07, *p* = 0.15). At the population level this appears as a near-monotonic trend: Gaviota, the lowest-fog-variability population, is the only population with a confidently negative inversion Δ*D* (−0.34, 95% CI −0.50 to −0.12); the three intermediate populations (Tranquillon Mtn, Pt. Conception, Lion's Head) cluster near zero; and Pt. Arguello, the highest-fog-variability population, has the largest and most confidently positive inversion Δ*D* (+0.45, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.60), essentially matching rather than falling below its general-outlier Δ*D*. The general "Outliers" category is positive in every population regardless of fog variability, suggesting a broad signature largely independent of the local environment, whereas the inversion category is the one that tracks fog variability specifically — the pattern predicted if the inversion is more responsive to the local selection regime than the rest of the outlier set, though still short of formal statistical significance at n = 5 populations.

A sensitivity check using the longer-baseline Kling fog record's detrended SD (`Kling_detrend`) in place of `fog_detrend` was also attempted but discarded: `Kling_detrend` ranked the five populations quite differently from `fog_detrend` (e.g. Pt. Conception moved from mid-pack to the highest-variability population, and Lion's Head/Pt. Arguello — clearly distinct under `fog_detrend` — were nearly tied), and its narrow range across populations (0.030–0.063) produced uninformative, extremely wide-CI slope estimates. Given this disagreement, Werner FLCC-derived `fog_detrend` was retained as the sole basis for the fog-variability mixed model rather than treating `Kling_detrend` as a corroborating check.
